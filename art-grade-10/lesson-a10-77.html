Term 3 · Lesson 7 · Practical lesson

Activity 1 (part 1): proportions of the figure

Drawing: graphite, charcoal, line, perspective

Start hereSlidesNotesPracticalTest yourselfFinished early?

Start here

Today I will

  • Use the head as a unit to measure the figure
  • Place the key landmarks of the body
  • Plan poses with a simple mannequin figure
Think first

How many of your own heads tall do you think you are?

Words to Know

WordMeaning
Canon of proportionA set of rules for the ideal measurements of the human body.
Head unitThe height of the head, used to measure the rest of the body.
LandmarkA point on the body used for measuring, such as the navel or the knee.
MannequinA simple jointed figure used to plan a pose.
Vitruvian ManLeonardo da Vinci's drawing (about 1490) of the ideal proportions of the body.

Check these words in your Art Theory or Art History booklet.

Notes

The figure about seven and a half to eight heads tall; its key landmarks; Leonardo's Vitruvian Man as a Renaissance study of proportion; the mannequin figure for planning poses.

The eight-head figure

  1. Divide: Draw a vertical line and divide it into eight equal head units.
  2. Head and chest: Head in the first unit; the chest line near the end of the second.
  3. Navel and hips: The navel near the end of the third; the hips at about the middle of the figure.
  4. Hands and knees: Fingertips reach the middle of the thigh; knees near the end of the sixth unit.
  5. Feet: The feet finish the eighth unit.

The mannequin figure

  • Ovals: Simple ovals for the head, ribcage and pelvis.
  • Lines: Lines for the spine and limbs.
  • Circles: Small circles for the joints: shoulders, elbows, hips, knees.
  • Proportion: Keep the limb lengths the same when the pose changes.
Remember

Measure with the head, plan with the mannequin.

Practical

On your own · about 30 minutes

You need: VJ, HB and 2B pencils, ruler

Activity 1 (part 1): proportions of the figure

  1. Draw a standing figure eight head units tall and label the landmarks.
  2. Mark the chest line, navel, hips, fingertips, knees and feet.
  3. Draw a mannequin standing, then sitting, then reaching.
  4. Keep the limb lengths the same in each pose.
  5. Annotate how the proportions seem to change when the figure bends or turns.
  6. Note one thing you noticed in Leonardo's Vitruvian Man.

Test yourself

  1. About how many heads tall is an adult figure?
  2. Where do the fingertips reach on a standing figure?
  3. Why use a mannequin figure?
Show answers

1. About seven and a half to eight. 2. About the middle of the thigh. 3. To plan a pose and keep the proportions right before adding detail.

Finished early?

Measure yourself (about 10 minutes)

Test the eight-head rule.

  1. Ask a partner to help you measure your head height.
  2. Measure your height in head units.
  3. Compare with the eight-head figure.
  4. Note one difference.
