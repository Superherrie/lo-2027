Activity 6, Part A: torn-paper tone and form

Painting and colour

Start hereSlidesNotesPracticalTest yourselfFinished early?

Start here

Today I will

  • Sort coloured paper by tone
  • Use torn paper to build light, mid-tone and shadow
  • Make my fruit or vegetable look three-dimensional
Think first

Can you 'paint' without paint? How could a pile of magazine scraps become light and shadow?

Words to know

Collage
Artwork made by sticking pieces of paper, fabric or other materials onto a surface.
Tone
How light or dark an area is.
Form
The three-dimensional quality of an object, shown through light and shade.
Layering
Building an area up with overlapping pieces, one on top of another.
Torn edge
The soft, uneven edge of torn paper, which blends more naturally than a cut edge.

Check these words in your Art Theory or Art History booklet.

Notes

Building tone and form with torn coloured paper, like painting with paper.

Painting with paper

  • Sort by value: Squint and sort scraps into light, middle and dark piles; colour matters less than tone.
  • Map the tones: Lightly shade on your contour where the light, mid and dark areas fall.
  • Darks and lights first: Glue the darkest and lightest areas first so the form appears early.
  • Fill and layer: Fill the mid-tones and overlap pieces to blend from one value to the next.
  • Follow the form: Tear and place pieces in the direction the surface curves, like brushstrokes.

What makes it convincing

  • Clear light source: All highlights on the same side; all shadows opposite.
  • Gradual change: Many small steps from light to dark look rounder than a few big jumps.
  • Edges: Torn edges blend inside the form; use sharper edges only along the contour.
  • Cast shadow: A dark shadow on the surface below anchors the object.
Remember

Tear, sort by tone, and let light and shadow build the form.

Practical

On your own · about 30 minutes

You need: Coloured paper scraps, glue stick, Part A contour drawing, phone

Activity 6, Part A: torn-paper tone and form

  1. Sort your scraps into light, middle and dark piles on scrap paper.
  2. Mark the light direction and lightly map the three tone areas on your drawing.
  3. Tear and glue the darkest areas, then the brightest highlights.
  4. Fill the mid-tones, overlapping to blend the transitions.
  5. Add the cast shadow and check the whole piece from a distance.
  6. Photograph the stage and annotate your VJ: how did you choose and place your scraps?

Test yourself

  1. Why is tone more important than colour when sorting scraps?
  2. Why do torn edges often work better than cut edges inside the form?
  3. What anchors an object so it does not float?
Show answers

1. Form is shown by light and dark, so the value of each scrap decides where it goes. 2. Soft, uneven torn edges blend from one tone into the next. 3. A cast shadow on the surface below it.

Finished early?

Three-value scrap strip (about 10 minutes)

Practise sorting by value, not colour.

  1. Choose six scraps of paper in different colours.
  2. Squint and arrange them in a row from lightest to darkest.
  3. Tear and glue them as a small tonal strip in your VJ, overlapping the edges.
  4. Write one sentence on which scrap surprised you once you squinted.
