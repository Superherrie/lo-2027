Experimental printmaking 1

Printmaking: linocut

Today I will

  • Make prints without a press using three methods
  • Create positive and negative impressions
  • Annotate my experiments in my VJ
Think first

Press your thumb on an inked surface and then on paper. Is the print the same as your thumb, or its mirror image?

Words to know

Monoprint
A print made from an inked surface that can only produce one unique image.
Collagraph
A print made from a plate built up like a collage from card, string and textured materials.
Impression
A single print pulled from an inked surface.
Brayer
A small rubber roller used to spread ink evenly on a plate or object.
Edition
A set of identical prints from the same plate, numbered, for example 3/10.

Check these words in your Art Theory or Art History booklet.

Notes

Printmaking without a press: monoprint, collagraph and found-object printing; positive and negative impressions.

Three ways to print without a press

  • Monoprint: draw into ink: Roll ink on the plate, draw into it with a cotton bud or stick, lay paper on top and rub.
  • Monoprint: trace: Lay paper on a thin layer of ink and draw on the back with a pencil; the pressure picks up a soft line.
  • Found-object print: Roll ink onto a leaf, lace or bubble wrap, place it on paper and press with a clean sheet on top.
  • Collagraph: Glue or tape string, mesh and textured card onto a card base. Ink it with the brayer and burnish a print.

Positive and negative impressions

  • Ink the plate: Roll a thin, even layer of ink on the plate until it sounds like a soft hiss.
  • Mask with an object: Lay a leaf or cut card shape on the inked plate and print: the shape stays white (a negative image).
  • Lift and print again: Lift the object off; its inked surface printed on fresh paper gives a positive image.
  • Ghost print: Print the plate once more without re-inking for a pale, soft second impression.
Remember

In printmaking, what is raised takes the ink, what is covered stays white, and every print comes out in reverse.

Practical

On your own · about 30 minutes

You need: Printing ink, brayers, inking plates, card, found objects, newsprint, cartridge paper, wooden spoons, VJ

A test sheet of six printing experiments

  1. Divide a sheet of cartridge paper into six boxes.
  2. Box 1 and 2: a draw-into-ink monoprint and a trace monoprint.
  3. Box 3: a found-object print (positive).
  4. Box 4: a masked print with the same object (negative), then a ghost print in box 5.
  5. Box 6: a quick collagraph from the card base and textured materials.
  6. Label each box and annotate in your VJ: method, what worked, what you would change.

Test yourself

  1. What makes a monoprint different from a collagraph print?
  2. How do you make a negative impression with a leaf?
  3. What does the number 3/10 on a print mean?
Show answers

1. A monoprint produces only one unique image, while a collagraph plate can be re-inked to make several similar prints. 2. Place the leaf on the inked plate and print over it, so the leaf's shape stays white on the paper. 3. It is the third print in an edition of ten.

Finished early?

Plan a collagraph plate (about 10 minutes)

Design a printing plate on paper before you build one.

  1. Draw a rectangle in your VJ as your plate.
  2. Plan where you would glue string, mesh and textured card, and label each material.
  3. Shade lightly to show which areas would print dark and which would stay white.
  4. Write your initials as they would need to appear on the plate so they print the right way round.
