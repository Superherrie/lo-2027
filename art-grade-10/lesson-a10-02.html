PAT 1: unpacking the brief

PAT: practical assessment task

Today I will

  • Read a PAT brief closely and pick out the theme
  • Find every hand-in date and activity
  • Understand how the rubric rewards process as well as the final work
Think first

A client gives a designer a brief for a new logo. What would go wrong if the designer only read the first line?

Words to know

Brief
The written instructions for a project: what to make, how and by when.
Theme
The big idea or topic that every part of the project must respond to.
Process
All the research, sketching and experimenting you do before and during the final work.
Final product
The finished artwork you hand in at the end of the project.
Rubric
The marking grid that shows what is assessed and what each level of work looks like.
Rationale
A short written explanation of your idea, your process and your influences.

Check these words in your Art Theory or Art History booklet.

Notes

Reading a PAT brief like an artist: the theme, the activities, the hand-in dates and the rubric; what 'process' and 'final product' mean; how marks are earned for research, experimenting and the final work.

Reading a brief like an artist

  1. Find the theme: Underline the big idea. Every activity and the final work must link back to it.
  2. List the activities: Number each task in the brief so you know how many pieces you must hand in.
  3. Mark the dates: Write every deadline in your diary and in the back of your VJ.
  4. Read the rubric: See what each mark is for: research, experiments, skill, idea and presentation.
  5. Write your questions: Anything unclear goes on a list so it can be answered before you start.

Process versus final product

ProcessFinal product
Mind maps, research and reference images.One resolved artwork that answers the theme.
Experiments with media, including the ones that failed.Shows the skill you built during the experiments.
Annotations: what worked, what did not, why.Supported by a short rationale.
Marked in the VJ throughout the project.Marked at the hand-in date.
Remember

Read the brief twice and the rubric once more: marks hide in the details.

Practical

On your own · about 30 minutes

You need: PAT 1 brief, highlighters

Highlight the brief

  1. Choose three highlighter colours and write a key at the top of the brief.
  2. Colour 1: highlight the theme and all the key instruction words.
  3. Colour 2: highlight every date and deadline.
  4. Colour 3: highlight anything you do not understand or want to ask about.
  5. Write the theme in your own words in one sentence at the bottom of the brief.
  6. List your questions on a sticky note or in your VJ to bring to the next lesson.

Test yourself

  1. What is the theme of PAT 1, in your own words?
  2. What is the difference between process and the final product?
  3. What is a rubric for?
Show answers

1. Learners' own wording of the PAT 1 theme. 2. Process is the research, sketches and experiments; the final product is the finished artwork. 3. It shows what is assessed and what each level of work looks like.
