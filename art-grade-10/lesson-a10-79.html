Term 3 · Lesson 9 · Practical lesson

Activity 3: a figure in action

Drawing: graphite, charcoal, line, perspective

Start hereSlidesNotesPracticalTest yourselfFinished early?

Start here

Today I will

  • Choose a strong action pose from my own photos
  • Show weight, balance and twist in the figure
  • Draw foreshortened limbs convincingly
Think first

Stand on one leg and lean forward. What did your body do to stop you falling?

Words to Know

WordMeaning
WeightHow heavy a body or a part of it looks, and where that heaviness rests.
BalanceHow a body stays upright or falls, depending on where its weight sits.
Centre of gravityThe point where the body's weight is concentrated, usually near the hips.
ForeshorteningThe way a part of the body coming towards you looks shorter and wider.
TwistThe turn of the ribcage against the pelvis in an active pose.

Check these words in your Art Theory or Art History booklet.

Slides

1 / 7

Notes

Weight, balance, twist and foreshortening; choosing the strongest frame from your own action photos; planning the A3 graphite drawing from a line of action and a mannequin.

Reading an action pose

  • Weight: Which foot or hand carries the weight?
  • Balance: Draw a line down from the centre of gravity: is the figure stable or falling?
  • Twist: Do the shoulders and hips face different ways?
  • Foreshortening: Which limb comes towards you and must look shorter?

From photo to A3

  1. Line of action: Find it in the photo and draw it large on the A3 sheet.
  2. Mannequin: Build the ovals and joints on the line of action, checking proportion with head units.
  3. Overlaps: For foreshortened limbs, draw overlapping shapes instead of long outlines.
  4. Forms: Turn the mannequin into the body's forms, then add tone from one light direction.
Remember

Find the weight, and the movement will follow.

Practical

On your own · about 30 minutes

You need: Own action photos, A3 cartridge, pencils 2H to 8B, eraser

Activity 3: a figure in action

  1. Choose your strongest action photo and say why in one sentence.
  2. Answer the four questions (weight, balance, twist, foreshortening) beside it in your VJ.
  3. Draw the line of action large on the A3 sheet.
  4. Build the mannequin on it and check the proportions.
  5. Turn the mannequin into forms, starting with the largest.
  6. Annotate one decision you made and why.

Test yourself

  1. What is foreshortening?
  2. Where is the centre of gravity of a standing body?
  3. Why look for twist in an action pose?
Show answers

1. A part of the body coming towards you looks shorter and wider. 2. Usually near the hips. 3. The turn between the ribcage and the pelvis makes a pose look active and alive.

Finished early?

Foreshortened hand (about 10 minutes)

A hand coming towards you.

  1. Point your hand at your face and look at it in a mirror or straight on.
  2. Draw the overlapping shapes, not the outline.
  3. Make the nearest fingers bigger.
  4. Add tone to show what is closest.
