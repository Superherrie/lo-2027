Term 3 · Lesson 9 · Practical lesson

Portrait anatomy 1: the eye and the nose

Drawing: graphite, charcoal, line, perspective

Start hereSlidesNotesPracticalTest yourselfFinished early?

Start here

Today I will

  • Understand the structure and proportions of the eye and nose
  • Draw them as three-dimensional forms using tone
  • Make step-by-step studies for PAT 3
Think first

When you draw an eye from memory, why does it often look like a lemon with a circle in it?

Words to know

Proportion
The size of one part compared to another, such as the eye to the whole head.
Iris
The coloured ring of the eye, with the dark pupil in the centre.
Eyelid
The fold of skin that wraps over the eyeball and has its own thickness.
Bridge
The upper, bony part of the nose between the eyes.
Nostril
The opening on the underside of the nose, shaped by the soft wings around it.
Form
Something with height, width and depth, shown in a drawing through tone.

Check these words in your Art Theory or Art History booklet.

Notes

The structure and proportions of the eye and nose; drawing them as forms, not symbols.

Drawing the eye as a form

  1. The ball: Start with a light circle: the eyeball sits in a socket, so lids wrap around a sphere.
  2. The lids: Draw the upper lid with a high curve and thickness; the lower lid is flatter and gentler.
  3. Iris and pupil: The upper lid usually covers the top of the iris. Keep the circle round, not an oval.
  4. Tone: Shade the white of the eye slightly darker at the corners, and leave one highlight in the iris.
  5. Lashes and brow: Add last, in groups, curving in the direction they grow. Do not outline every lash.

Drawing the nose as a form

  1. Simple blocks: See the nose as a wedge: the bridge, a rounded ball at the tip and two wings at the sides.
  2. Light and shadow: Shade one side of the bridge and the underside of the nose, which faces away from the light.
  3. Nostrils: Nostrils are dark shapes inside the underside, not two black dots. Vary their shape and tone.
  4. No hard outline: Show the edge of the nose through tone changes, not a dark line down the side.
Remember

Draw the eye as a ball and the nose as a block, and let tone do the rest.

Practical

On your own · about 30 minutes

You need: Hand mirror, A3 cartridge paper, HB, 2B and 4B pencils, putty eraser, VJ

Step-by-step eye and nose studies

  1. Divide your paper into two halves: eye and nose.
  2. Using the mirror, draw the eye in five small stages across the top half, from ball to lashes.
  3. Then draw one larger, finished eye with a full range of tone.
  4. In the bottom half, draw the nose in four stages, from simple blocks to finished tone.
  5. Check proportion: is the iris the right size compared to the eye opening?
  6. Annotate in your VJ: two things you noticed in the mirror that surprised you.

Test yourself

  1. Why does the upper eyelid usually cover part of the iris?
  2. How do you show the edge of the nose without a hard line?
  3. What shape should you start with when drawing an eye?
Show answers

1. The lid wraps over the round eyeball, and in a relaxed eye it rests over the top of the iris. 2. With changes in tone: shadow on one side and under the tip against lighter skin. 3. A sphere (a circle for the eyeball).

Finished early?

Eye in five stages (about 10 minutes)

Build an eye as a form, step by step.

  1. Draw a light circle for the eyeball in your VJ.
  2. Wrap the upper and lower lids around it, giving the upper lid thickness.
  3. Add the iris partly covered by the upper lid, and the pupil.
  4. Shade the corners of the white of the eye and leave one highlight in the iris.
