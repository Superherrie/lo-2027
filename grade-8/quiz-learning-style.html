How Do I Learn Best?

About 5 to 8 minutes · nothing you tick is saved or sent · back to the lesson

Thabo learnt his new basketball moves by doing them again and again. Mia remembers things best when she draws them in colour. Aarav likes to talk a new idea through with a friend. How do YOU learn best? Answer these 12 everyday questions to find out.

How to do it

  1. Read each question and tick the answer that sounds most like you.
  2. You may tick more than one answer if more than one fits. Leave a question blank if none fit.
  3. There are no right or wrong answers. Be honest: tick what you really do, not what you think you should do.
  4. Click See my result at the end. Write your top one or two styles in your LO book.
1 You get a new game or app and you are not sure how it works. What do you do?
2 A friend asks how to get to your house. How do you help?
3 You have to learn the parts of a plant cell for Natural Sciences. What helps most?
4 Your coach is teaching the team a new move. What helps you get it right?
5 You want to bake something new for a cake sale. How do you go about it?
6 A teacher is explaining something new in class. What helps you understand it?
7 You must learn ten new vocabulary words for a test. What do you do?
8 You are choosing a new pair of sports shoes. What helps you decide?
9 Your group is doing a project. Which job would you choose?
10 It is the weekend and you have free time. What would you most like to do?
11 You need to remember what happened in a story or a History lesson. What works for you?
12 Think of a test that went really well. How did you study for it?

All four styles and their study tips

Visual (seeing)

You learn best from what you can see: pictures, diagrams, colours, maps and patterns on the page.

  • Turn your notes into a mind map with a colour for each main idea.
  • Draw a quick sketch, timeline or flow chart next to each heading.
  • Make flash cards with a small picture or symbol on the front.

Aural (listening)

You learn best from what you hear and say: explanations, discussions, rhymes and talking things through.

  • Explain the work out loud, as if you are teaching it to someone else.
  • Record yourself reading your summary and listen to it again later.
  • Study with a partner and ask each other questions.

Read/write (reading and writing)

You learn best from words on a page: reading notes, writing lists and putting ideas into your own sentences.

  • Rewrite each section of your notes in your own words, shorter each time.
  • Turn headings into questions and write out the answers from memory.
  • Make neat lists of key words with their meanings.

Kinaesthetic (doing)

You learn best by doing: moving, building, trying things out and linking work to real life.

  • Walk around or use your hands (sort flash cards into piles) while you revise.
  • Act out a scenario or build a simple model of what you are learning.
  • Link every idea to a real example from your own life, then practise with past questions.
