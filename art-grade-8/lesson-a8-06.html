Reflective surfaces

Drawing: graphite, charcoal, line, perspective

Start hereSlidesNotesPracticalTest yourselfFinished early?

Start here

Today I will

  • Today I will see how shiny surfaces differ from textured ones
  • Today I will use high contrast and hard and soft edges
  • Today I will draw two reflective objects from life
Think first

Look at yourself in the back of a spoon, then the front. Why do you look so strange?

Words to know

Reflective surface
A shiny, smooth surface (metal, glass, water) that shows light and the things around it.
Reflection
The mirrored and often bent image of the surroundings on a shiny surface.
Hard edge
A sharp, clear border between two tones.
Soft edge
A gentle, blurred change from one tone to another.
High contrast
A big difference between the lightest lights and darkest darks, side by side.
Distortion
When a shape looks bent or stretched, like your face in the back of a spoon.

Find these words in your Art Vocabulary booklet.

Notes

How shiny objects (a spoon, a metal cup) show high contrast, sharp highlights, distorted reflections, and hard and soft edges; the difference between texture and shine.

Texture versus shine

Textured surfaceShiny (reflective) surface
Many small marks repeatedSmooth, blended tone with no visible marks
Soft, gentle changes in toneSudden jumps from very dark to bright white
Highlights spread outSmall, sharp highlights
Shows its own surfaceShows the room around it, bent and stretched

How to draw shine

  • Outline lightly: Draw the shape with a light HB line; check the proportions.
  • Map the shapes: Lightly outline the shapes of light and dark you see inside the object.
  • Save the highlights: Leave them as white paper, or lift them with a putty eraser.
  • Go very dark: Use 4B or 6B beside the highlights for high contrast.
  • Edges: Keep some edges hard and sharp, blend others softly.
Remember

Shine is not a colour: it is darks and lights placed side by side.

Practical

On your own · about 37 minutes

You need: Pencils (HB to 6B), putty eraser, blending stump, spoons and metal cups, desk lamp

Two reflective studies

  1. Place a spoon and a metal cup in front of you with the lamp on one side.
  2. Draw the spoon lightly, about the size of your hand.
  3. Map the light and dark shapes inside it.
  4. Shade smoothly, saving the white highlights and pushing the darks.
  5. Repeat with the metal cup.
  6. Write one sentence under each: where did you use a hard edge and where a soft edge?

Test yourself

  1. Why does a reflection on a spoon look bent?
  2. How do you keep a highlight bright?
  3. What does high contrast mean?
Show answers

1. The curved surface distorts (bends) the reflection. 2. Leave the white paper untouched or lift it out with a putty eraser. 3. Very light and very dark tones placed right next to each other.

Finished early?

Hard and soft edge swatches (about 8 minutes)

Practise the two kinds of edge that make shine look real.

  1. Draw two small rectangles in your visual journal.
  2. In the first, put a very dark 6B area right next to white paper with a sharp border (hard edge).
  3. In the second, blend from dark to white gradually with a stump (soft edge).
  4. Lift one tiny sharp highlight out of the dark area with a putty eraser.
