Think first

If you could only use a pencil, how would you show Van Gogh's bright yellow stars?

Words to know

Mark-making
All the different marks a tool can make: dots, dashes, swirls and scribbles.
Directional line
A line that leads the eye in a direction, such as around a swirl.
Value
How light or dark something is.
Detail
A small, chosen part of a larger artwork.

Find these words in your Art Vocabulary booklet.

Notes

Value and mark-making: translate a section of The Starry Night into graphite marks (directional lines, swirls, value).

From paint to pencil

In the paintingIn graphite
Bright yellow stars and moonWhite paper or very light HB marks
Deep blue skyDark 4B and 6B marks
Swirling brushstrokesCurved, directional lines that follow the swirl
Dark cypressThe darkest flame-like strokes
Remember

In graphite, colour becomes value and brushstrokes become marks.

Practical

On your own · about 40 minutes

You need: Pencils (HB to 6B), print of the painting, viewfinder, journal

Starry Night detail in graphite

  1. Use a viewfinder to choose a small detail of the painting.
  2. Draw a box in your journal and lightly outline the main shapes.
  3. Leave the lightest areas white.
  4. Fill the rest with directional marks that follow Van Gogh's strokes.
  5. Press harder or change to a softer pencil for the darkest areas.

Test yourself

  1. How did you show the bright stars?
  2. Which pencil did you use for the darkest areas?
  3. What is a directional line?
Show answers

1. Left the paper white or used very light marks. 2. A 4B or 6B (own answer). 3. A line that leads the eye in a direction.
